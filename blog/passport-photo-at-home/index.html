How to Take a Passport Photo at Home That Gets Accepted

Travel Document Vault

April 29, 2026 · 7 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Use a plain, light background in your authority's required shade, neutral expression, and eyes open and looking straight ahead, with exceptions for young children
  • Size requirements differ from country to country and are enforced strictly, so take the measurements from your own passport authority rather than any summary
  • Natural window light works best - avoid harsh shadows and avoid using camera flash
  • Many authorities, including the UK's, ask you not to wear glasses unless you have to - check current requirements
  • Photos can be rejected for failing your authority's rules on lighting, size, expression, clarity or background

GOV.UK says booth or shop photos are more likely to be approved than own-device photos. Photos can be rejected for failing your authority's rules on lighting, size, expression, clarity or background.

Photo Requirements Vary by Authority

Photo requirements vary by passport authority, application format and age group.

Background and Composition

You need a plain, light background, and the exact shade depends on your authority: GOV.UK generally asks for plain cream or light grey for printed photos, while the Government of Canada asks for plain white or light-coloured. No patterns, shadows, blurred backgrounds, or strong colours. The reasoning is straightforward: officials need a clean, uncluttered view of your face to compare against future documents. Use a plain sheet or poster board in the shade your authority asks for (cream or light grey for UK printed photos), or even paint a small section of wall. The key is keeping it bright and uniform throughout.

Expression and Pose

Your face generally needs to fill the frame to the proportion your passport authority specifies, with eyes open and clearly visible. GOV.UK allows expression exceptions for children under 6 and open-eye exceptions for children under one. Look straight ahead at the camera with a neutral expression, tilting your head slightly if that feels more natural, but generally keeping your head square to the lens. Your ears should ideally be visible on both sides of your face.

Can You Wear Glasses in Your Passport Photo?

Rules on regular glasses vary more than you might expect. GOV.UK tells UK applicants not to wear them unless they have to. The Government of Canada accepts them as long as your eyes are clearly visible and there's no glare on the lenses. Religious headgear is typically permitted as long as it does not obscure the facial features needed for identification.

Sunglasses and tinted lenses are generally refused. If you do qualify to keep your glasses on, angle your head slightly away from the light so the lenses carry no reflection, and check that the frames do not cross any part of your eyes.

Passport Photo Size: the Two Standards Most Countries Use

Printed photo sizes vary by authority. The UK uses 35mm x 45mm and the Government of Canada asks for printed photos 50mm wide by 70mm high. Other countries set their own sizes, so take yours from your own passport authority.

Digital submissions swap millimetres for pixels, and every authority publishes its own minimums. Those two sizes are what most authorities currently ask for in print, but specifications get revised, so confirm yours before you print or upload.

Where to Check Your Country’s Size Requirements

Size matters: your photo can be rejected if it doesn't match your country's requirements. Every country sets its own, some in inches and some in millimetres, and they get revised from time to time.

We deliberately don't reprint those numbers here. A figure copied into a blog post is a figure that can quietly go out of date, and this post is not the authority on it. Check your own passport office directly.

Lighting and Image Quality

Lighting problems can lead to rejection. Shadows across your face, uneven illumination, or blown-out areas around the face can disqualify the photo.

Best Lighting Practices

Window light is your best bet. Position yourself perpendicular to the window for even illumination across your face and to avoid harsh shadows under the eyes or along one side. Overcast days work particularly well because clouds diffuse the light evenly without the harshness of direct sun. Avoid shooting at midday in full sunlight, which tends to create unflattering shadows and makes you squint.

Never use your phone's camera flash. Flash tends to create harsh shadows, washes out skin tone, and often causes red-eye. If your room is dark, move closer to the window or choose a different time of day. We'd retake the photo in good light before submitting it.

Use a tripod or prop your phone against a stable object so you have both hands free to position your face correctly. This also ensures the camera is level and focused on your face. If you must hold the phone, have someone else take the photo to avoid camera shake and uneven framing.

Resolution and Focus

Most modern smartphone cameras have plenty of resolution for a passport photo. Before you shoot, make sure your phone is in focus mode: tap on your face on the screen, and most phones will lock focus there. Your final image should be sharp and clear.

Photo Problems and How to Avoid Them

  • Shadows on the face: side lighting or a harsh light source is usually the cause, so position yourself perpendicular to a window for even illumination and check that your ears and cheekbones are lit evenly.
  • Incorrect photo dimensions: Photo does not match your country's specifications (requirements vary by country). For UK own-device digital photos, GOV.UK tells applicants not to crop them.
  • Smiling or unusual expression: GOV.UK generally requires a neutral expression, with an exception for children under 6. We'd practise a calm, straight-ahead look in a mirror beforehand: aim for serious, not stern.
  • Glasses with glare: Glare on lenses obscures your eyes. Either remove your glasses or adjust the angle to eliminate reflection. Some authorities, including the UK's, ask you not to wear glasses unless you have to, so check your authority's current rules first.
  • Blurry or out-of-focus image: Motion or focus issues during capture. Use a tripod or stable object to prop your phone, tap the screen to focus on your face, and avoid any movement during the shot.
  • Wrong background: the usual culprit is a coloured background, a pattern, or an uneven white, so switch to a plain, light poster board or bedsheet in the shade your authority asks for with no visible texture or shadow.
  • Excessive head room or cropping: Face too small or positioned incorrectly in frame. Your country's rules will state exactly how much of the frame your face should fill, usually as a chin-to-crown measurement rather than a percentage - work to that figure, not to what looks right.

Prepare Your Smartphone Photo for Submission

GOV.UK tells applicants not to crop own-device digital photos; UK printed photos must generally not be cut down from a larger picture.

Cropping Tools

We'd follow the authority's format instructions before using a cropping tool. GOV.UK tells applicants not to crop own-device digital photos.

Printing vs. Digital Submission

Some countries accept digital uploads of the biometric passport photo submitted online, while others still want a printed physical photo, so check your passport authority's current submission process before you take yours. If printing, use a professional photo printer or take your digital file to a pharmacy or print shop. Home inkjet printers typically produce acceptable results, but colour accuracy and paper quality matter. Photo paper is inexpensive and ensures better colour fidelity than standard printer paper.

Preparation Checklist

If your authority allows a home photo, ensure you have the following in place:

  • Plain, light background material in your authority's required shade, ready and positioned
  • Good natural light source (preferably a window)
  • Smartphone with a functioning camera
  • Tripod or stable object to prop your phone
  • Knowledge of your country's exact photo specifications
  • Your authority's instructions on cropping
  • Plan for printing or digital submission based on your country's requirements

If your authority allows home photos and you've got decent window light, we'd try taking yours at home. If you're not confident in your setup, or you're racing a deadline, we'd ask a photo provider about turnaround and whether they follow your authority's photo rules.

Keep your own passport and visa dates on your phone, with reminders before they expire.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I take a passport photo on my smartphone

GOV.UK generally allows own-device digital passport photos, while Canada generally requires a commercial photographer. Position yourself in front of a plain, light background in the shade your passport authority asks for, with good natural light, and ensure the photo meets your country's size and quality requirements. GOV.UK tells applicants not to crop own-device digital photos.

What size should a passport photo be

Printed sizes vary by authority. The UK uses 35mm x 45mm and the Government of Canada uses 50mm x 70mm. Other countries set their own sizes, so take yours from your own passport authority. Digital submissions have separate pixel minimums. Requirements are enforced strictly and get revised, so confirm the exact spec with your own passport authority before you print or upload.

Why was my passport photo rejected

Photos can be rejected for failing your authority's rules on lighting, size, expression, clarity or background. Glasses rules depend on the authority. Ensure your photo meets all official requirements before submission. Review the specific feedback from your passport authority and retake the photo addressing each issue mentioned.

Do I need special lighting for a passport photo

Natural light from a window is ideal for passport photos. Avoid harsh shadows on your face and do not use harsh camera flash. Position yourself facing the light source directly or perpendicular to a window so the light falls evenly across your face. Overcast days provide ideal diffused light without harsh shadows.

Can I wear glasses in a passport photo

It depends on the authority. GOV.UK tells UK applicants not to wear glasses unless they have to, while the Government of Canada accepts them if your eyes are clearly visible and there's no glare on the lenses. Sunglasses and tinted lenses are generally refused. Check your country's current requirements before taking your photo, as rules have evolved in recent years. When in doubt, take both a photo with and without glasses to have options.

What are the rules for a passport photo?

Rules vary by authority, application format and age group. GOV.UK generally requires a plain cream or light grey background for printed photos, but allows exceptions to expression rules for children under 6 and open-eye rules for children under one.
