International Travel Document Checklist: Everything You Need Before You Fly

Travel Document Vault

March 4, 2026 · 9 min read

Key Takeaways

  • The most useful travel document checklist is organised by when you need to act - not by document type. Sorting by stage means you catch problems while there's still time to fix them.
  • Passports need checking at booking time, not the night before. A passport with under six months left can get you turned away for some destinations, so check the exact figure for yours at booking.
  • Family travel adds a separate layer: every child needs their documents checked individually, and a parental consent letter may be needed if one parent isn't travelling.
  • Business travellers often carry multiple passports and work visas - these need their own tracking, separate from personal documents.
  • A digital backup of every document (encrypted, not in your photo library) could get you home faster if something goes wrong abroad.

Most travel document checklists are just a flat list: passport, visa, insurance. They tell you what to bring, but not when to check it, which is where people get caught out. The family who realise at check-in that one passport expires in five months, the solo traveller who booked without noticing their visa requirement, or the business traveller who forgot which passport their visa is tied to - these aren't edge cases, they're the norm.

This checklist is organised by trip stage instead. Starting from the moment you book and working through to security, each stage has a specific set of actions that need doing. Following them in order is how the usual last-minute scrambles don't happen.

At Booking: The Checks That Actually Matter

The biggest mistake travellers make is treating the booking moment as administrative and document checks as something to do later. These need to happen at exactly the same time.

Check your travel documents before you pay

Before you confirm any booking, check each passport you'll use against three criteria: validity, name match, and blank pages. Validity means meeting the destination's requirement: many countries want six months of validity left on the day you arrive, and a few count from departure, so check your destination, as explained in our guide to the 6-month passport rule. The name on the passport must exactly match the booking name, and some countries require at least two blank visa pages for entry stamps.

Check your visa requirements

Find out whether your passport nationality requires a visa for entry, transit, or both. Your government's official travel advice service and the IATA Travel Centre both answer this, the latter being the same database airlines use to verify passenger documents. You'll need to know if a visa is required, or whether you're eligible for visa-on-arrival or e-visa access, how far in advance application needs to happen, and whether the visa covers multiple entries. If the two documents still blur together, the difference between a visa and a passport is worth five minutes before you start.

Book your travel insurance early

Book travel insurance when you book flights, not a week before departure. Make sure the policy covers all travellers, all destinations, and the activities you plan to do.

Three Months Before: If Renewal or a Visa Is Close, Start Now

At this point you still have time to fix things. Renewal times change, and your country's passport authority publishes its own current figure. Always check the official site for current times before you apply.

We'd renew any passport expiring within 12 months now, because children's passports expire faster (UK child passports last 5 years, against 10 for adults, and several other countries work the same way) and parents frequently miss the window. For New Zealand applicants, check the New Zealand Passport Office for current timelines.

Submit any visa applications that need lead time, and check the issuing authority for how long it currently takes. Prepare supporting documents: bank statements, accommodation confirmations, onward travel proof. Book a biometrics appointment in advance if the visa requires it.

For families: if a child is travelling with only one parent or another adult, we'd prepare a child travel consent letter now. Include the child's full name and date of birth, both parents' contact details, travel dates, destinations, and the absent parent's signature. Some destinations also ask for the letter to be notarised, so check the entry rules.

One Month Before: Confirm and Consolidate

By one month before travel, all your documents should be in hand. This stage is about confirming they're correct and organising them where they're easy to grab.

Identity Documents

  • Passports or accepted national ID cards confirmed valid and in travel wallet
  • Visas confirmed (check validity dates, entry counts, any conditions)
  • National ID card if accepted at destination (EU travel within Schengen)

Travel Documents

  • Travel insurance certificate downloaded or printed
  • Booking confirmations: flights, hotels, car hire
  • Vaccination certificates if required by destination
  • International driving permit if hiring a car abroad

Family and Special Documents

  • Parental consent letter prepared and signed if applicable
  • Birth certificates accessible if required by destination country
  • Medical records or prescriptions for ongoing conditions

For business travellers: if you hold two passports, confirm which passport your visa is attached to and which you need to enter and exit each country. Some countries require you to enter and exit on the same document, so getting this wrong can turn a minor mistake into a border delay.

The Night Before: Final Confirmation

These are confirmations, not discoveries.

If something's missing now, you've got hours to sort it out, not weeks.

Night Before

  • Passports or accepted national ID cards out and accounted for - one per person
  • Boarding passes downloaded to phone or printed
  • Travel insurance certificate accessible on phone
  • Hotel and transfer confirmations downloaded offline
  • Any required vaccination or health certificates
  • Parental consent letter, if applicable, in bag, not in suitcase
  • Emergency contact numbers written down somewhere offline

At the Airport: Keep These With You

Keep these in your carry-on at all times, not in checked luggage. That means passports, boarding passes, travel insurance certificate, parental consent letter if applicable, and any visa documentation or approval letters.

Before you leave home, take a photo of every document and store it in an encrypted app - not your camera roll. If your bag is lost or stolen, you'll want your passport number, insurance policy number, and booking references somewhere you can still access them at an embassy or police station. A secure digital backup saves hours when you're trying to get help.

How Your Document List Changes by Trip Type

Different trips need different document sets, as the table below shows for solo, family and business travel.

Visa (if required) Yes each member check visa or travel authorisation for planned activities Business: confirm visa is in the correct passport
Travel insurance Yes all covered check employer covers you Confirm coverage before each trip, not just at purchase
Booking confirmations Yes Yes Yes Offline copy (don't rely on email access at borders)
Child consent letter N/A if applicable N/A Asked for in some countries if one parent is absent
Birth certificates N/A if required N/A Some countries ask for this for children under 18 (for example, Mexico can ask for evidence of your relationship)

If a trip is already booked, the one thing worth doing today is pulling out every passport involved and checking its expiry against the destination's requirement, not just against your travel dates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for international travel?

The core documents to check for international travel are a valid passport or accepted national ID card, any required visa, travel insurance, and booking confirmations. For family travel, add each child's accepted travel document (checked individually), birth certificates if required by the destination, and a parental consent letter if needed. Business travellers should confirm which passport their work visa is attached to.

How far in advance should I check my travel documents?

Check at booking time - before you pay. Passport validity, visa requirements, and insurance all need confirming the moment you book. Three months before travel is the last comfortable window to renew a passport or apply for a visa on standard timelines. Night-before checks should be confirmations only - not discoveries.

Does every family member need their own travel document checklist?

Yes. Each person's passport expiry, visa requirements, and insurance coverage is assessed individually. One family member's documents can be in perfect order while another's fall short. Run the checklist for each person separately - this is where families most often get caught out.

What is a child travel consent letter and do I need one?

A child travel consent letter is a signed document from the absent parent giving permission for the child to travel. South Africa generally exempts accompanied foreign visa-exempt children from extra supporting documents, and Canadian authorities recommend a signed consent letter. Without one you may face extra questions or delays. The letter should include the child's details, both parents' contact information, travel dates, destinations, and the absent parent's signature.

What documents should I keep accessible at the airport and not in checked luggage?

Keep these with you at all times: passports, boarding passes, travel insurance certificate, parental consent letter if applicable, and any visa documentation or approval letters. Never put identity documents in checked luggage. A digital backup of key documents in a dedicated passport organiser app - not your camera roll - is strongly recommended in case anything is lost or stolen.
