ETIAS: What Documents You Need and How to Apply

Travel Document Vault

May 8, 2026 · 7 min read

Key Takeaways

  • ETIAS is a digital travel authorisation for UK, US, and Canadian travellers, required for visa-exempt entry to the Schengen area once it becomes mandatory
  • You need a passport valid for more than 3 months beyond your departure date and an email address to apply. It must also have been issued within the last ten years.
  • Processing time varies, especially soon after launch; apply well ahead of your trip and check the official EU site for current guidance
  • Check the official EU site for the current fee; under-18s and over-70s don't pay but still need authorisation
  • Doubts about the reliability of your answers can lead to refusal, so disclose everything, even if it's old

You've booked a family trip to Italy and you've heard that ETIAS is on its way, a new digital authorisation requirement for visa-exempt visitors to Europe. This guide covers what ETIAS is, what documents you need, and how the application works, so you're prepared before you reach the form.

The European Travel Information and Authorisation System (ETIAS) is not a visa. It's a digital pre-travel clearance for visitors from visa-exempt countries. If you're a UK, US, or Canadian citizen, you generally register at the border under the Entry/Exit System (photo and fingerprints at first entry) and pass a border check. Once ETIAS becomes mandatory, that process moves online, and you apply before you fly.

What Is ETIAS and Who Needs It?

ETIAS is a digital authorisation system designed to strengthen border security and travel management across the Schengen area. It applies to citizens of visa-exempt countries, meaning countries whose nationals can currently enter Europe without a visa for short stays. This includes the UK, USA, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, and dozens of others.

If you hold a visa such as a Schengen long-stay visa, UK family visa, or other residence permit, you won't need ETIAS; nor will EU citizens, Norwegian, Icelandic, and Liechtenstein nationals. Children under 18 and adults over 70 are exempt from the fee but still require authorisation at no cost.

ETIAS affects a wide range of travellers: individual holidaymakers, families applying separately for each member, and digital nomads planning repeated short stays. Remember that every person in your family needs their own ETIAS application, including children and older adults, who require authorisation even though they are exempt from the fee. If your family holds a mix of visas and visa-exempt passports, treat ETIAS as a question for each person, not for the whole booking.

Check Your Passport's Three-Month Buffer First

Unlike traditional visas, ETIAS requires no physical document submission. You apply entirely online instead, so have your information ready before you start the form.

Your passport must be valid for more than three months beyond your intended date of departure from Europe, and issued within the last ten years. It's an easy trap to fall into: a passport expiring on 30 December looks fine for a trip departing 1 December, but the EU asks for more than three months after departure. Beyond that validity window, it also needs to be a travel document recognised by the countries you visit.

You'll also need to provide an email address, which doubles as your login for tracking the application. ETIAS sends all status updates, approvals, and rejections to this inbox, so use one you check regularly. If your family shares email accounts, it's worth setting up individual addresses for each traveller to avoid missing critical notifications.

During the application the form asks about past criminal convictions, travel to war or conflict zones and any return decision. What matters is giving consistent answers, which helps prevent problems when the system cross-checks your information.

ETIAS will ask for security and personal information: your full name, date of birth, place of birth, nationality, and contact details, alongside questions about any criminal convictions or a return decision. Honesty matters here: mistakes or false information can lead to refusal, revocation or annulment of an authorisation.

The EU's list of what the form asks for includes your level of education and current occupation, not proof of income.

Many applicants mistakenly assume they need vaccination records, hotel bookings, or return flight confirmations. ETIAS does not require these at the application stage. You may need them for border inspection, but ETIAS approval does not depend on them.

List Every Country You Plan to Visit on the Form

Once you've gathered your information, the application itself is straightforward. Visit the official ETIAS portal and start a new application.

The form asks for your personal details (name, date of birth, nationality), passport information (number and validity date), and your travel plans (intended destination and stay duration). Be precise: if you plan to visit three countries, list all three. If you're unsure about exact dates, use approximate month ranges, because inconsistency is what triggers rejections, not approximate dates.

Answer every question accurately.

At the end, you review your information, pay the fee shown at checkout (free if under 18 or over 70), and submit. A confirmation number is generated immediately, and your application enters the processing queue.

Apply as Early as You Reasonably Can

The European Commission publishes current processing time guidance on its official site, and it's worth checking that before you assume you have time. Early demand after launch, background check delays, and any need to fix a rejection can all add to the wait, so apply as early as you reasonably can before your trip. Apply the moment your travel dates are locked in, not the week before departure.

An application is granted, refused, or the authorities ask for more information.

An approval means you're granted a digital ETIAS linked to your passport number. There's nothing to print or carry, as the system will recognise you automatically at the border. Your approval remains valid for three years, or until your passport expires, whichever comes first.

A request for more information can follow incomplete or inconsistent answers, such as missing travel dates, conflicting employment history or unclear responses. The EU gives you 10 additional days to provide what is asked, so reply promptly.

A refusal comes with its reasons and the right to appeal, handled by the authorities of the refusing country. You can also apply again: the EU says a previous refusal does not lead to the automatic refusal of a new application.

Disclose Everything, Even Old Convictions

One thing is worth getting right before you submit.

Undisclosed criminal convictions: If you have a criminal record and don't disclose it but ETIAS finds it during a background check, your application can be refused. Always disclose, even if the conviction is old or has been expunged in your home country, because European authorities may still retain records. Doubts about the reliability of the information you give are a stated ground for refusal, so disclose everything.

Review your information carefully before submitting and answer every question accurately.

Children Should Still Apply, Even Without a Fee

Children under 18 don't pay the ETIAS fee but should still apply for authorisation, with parents applying on their behalf.

Family groups must submit each application separately rather than as a single "family" unit.

If you're refused and still need to travel, you can appeal or apply again.

Your Passport and Insurance Still Matter After ETIAS

ETIAS approval does not replace your passport, travel insurance, or passport validity requirements, so you still need:

  • A valid passport (3+ months validity)
  • Travel insurance (advisable; border officers may ask about your funds and accommodation)
  • Proof of onward travel (return flight or onward ticket)
  • Proof of accommodation or travel itinerary
  • Sufficient funds for your stay

Border officers may still request any of these documents upon arrival, even with a valid ETIAS, since ETIAS merely speeds up the authorisation process rather than exempting you from standard border inspection and documentation requirements. That's worth remembering before you assume ETIAS alone gets you through the gate.

The practical task to do today is pull out your passport and check it against the validity rule covered above; every applicant needs that sorted before anything else on this list.

Keep your own passport and visa dates on your phone, with reminders before they expire.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does ETIAS become mandatory for European travel?

ETIAS has not yet been made mandatory, and the timetable has shifted several times. The Entry-Exit System (EES) is rolling out first, with ETIAS expected to follow once EES is established. There may be a transitional period where ETIAS operates in parallel with passport stamps. Check the official EU site for the current position, and apply several months before your planned travel to account for processing and rollout delays.

Who needs to apply for ETIAS?

ETIAS is required for visa-exempt non-EU citizens travelling for short-stay visits (up to 90 days in 180 days). Visitors from the USA, Canada, UK, and several other countries are included. Children under 18 and adults over 70 do not pay but still require authorisation. EU residents and those with long-stay visas do not require ETIAS.

What documents do I need to apply for ETIAS?

You must have a valid passport with more than three months validity beyond your intended departure date, issued within the last ten years. You will also need an email address.

How much does ETIAS cost and how long does it take?

The European Commission publishes the current ETIAS fee on its official site. Children under 18 and applicants over 70 do not pay, though they still need authorisation. The fee is non-refundable even if your application is rejected. Processing time varies, especially during the initial launch phase, so check the official guidance and apply well ahead of your trip rather than close to your departure date.

What happens if my ETIAS application is rejected?

If your application is refused, you will receive a notification explaining the reasons, and you have the right to appeal, handled by the authorities of the refusing country. You can also apply again: the EU says a previous refusal does not lead to the automatic refusal of a new application.

Can I travel to Europe before ETIAS launches?

Yes. ETIAS is not yet mandatory. While ETIAS is not yet required, visa-exempt travellers generally register at the border under the Entry/Exit System (photo and fingerprints at first entry) and pass a border check. Once ETIAS becomes mandatory, all future trips will require prior authorisation. Check the official EU site before booking to confirm the current position.
